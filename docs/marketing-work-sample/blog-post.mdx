| Problem | Solution |
| --- | --- |
| The title and opening named the topic but did not say the article explains why it matters. | The title and subtitle now state the explanation: unused permissions can leave integrations exposed, and removing them safely prevents that exposure. |
| Abstract phrases and metaphors ("the longest list", "the biggest doors", "the first screen", "not every exposure is equal") did not say what was being compared or where to look. | Each is a concrete statement, such as "the longest list of unused permissions", with a screenshot of the screen it describes. The section headings now say what each section covers. |
| The sections on safe removal and setup described the report ("the report recalculates once a day") and did not speak to a reader's situation. | One example, a security engineer reviewing the service account provisioning-runner, runs through both sections and shows what the reader sees and does. |
| Product terms and unclear references ("grants", "rights", "it", "they", "much") made sentences hard to follow. | The text says "permissions", repeats the noun where a pronoun was unclear, and bolds each name that appears in the product exactly as written, such as Access tab, Reach score and Deployment Operator. |
