## Troubleshooting

### Issue: I see a lot of "low severity" findings. Should I fix them all?

**Why this happens:** Low severity often means the agent has called a tool outside scope, but the tool has low risk (read-only, for example).

**How to handle:** Prioritize high and critical findings first. For low severity, batch them or monitor for patterns (if the same agent calls the same unauthorized tool repeatedly, that's worth addressing).

### Issue: A finding says my agent called a tool but I declared that in scope. Why is it flagged?

**Why this happens:** Scope might have been updated AFTER the call was made. Findings show the state at the time of the call, not today's state.

**How to handle:** Check the finding's timestamp. If it's before your scope update, it's a false positive—mark it "reviewed" and it won't reappear. If it's after your scope update, something is wrong with your scope declaration; contact your admin.

### Issue: The finding shows "agent not found" or is missing context.

**Why this happens:** The agent might have been deleted or deactivated. Findings from deleted agents become read-only.

**How to handle:** You can still mark the finding reviewed for audit purposes, but you can't take remediation action. No further action needed.

### Issue: I can't revoke access. The button is greyed out.

**Why this happens:** You might not have permission to modify agent access, or the agent is managed by a different team.

**How to handle:** Contact your platform admin or the team that manages the agent. You can still mark the finding reviewed and add a note about who to contact.

# Reviewing Unauthorized Access Findings

**What you'll accomplish:**  
Identify which tools an agent called outside its declared scope and decide whether to revoke access or update the scope declaration.

**Time required:**  
5 minutes per finding

**Audience:**  
Security teams, platform engineers with basic understanding of agent roles and tool scope

---

## Before you start

**Prerequisites:**
- You have a Ridgeline user account with access to the Findings dashboard
- You understand your organization's agent naming conventions
- You know the difference between "declared scope" (what the agent is allowed to call) and "actual access" (what it really called)

**You'll need:**
- Access to the Ridgeline dashboard
- Optional: your agent management tool (if you decide to update scope)

---

## Step 1: Find the Finding

1. Log into Ridgeline and navigate to **Findings** in the left menu
2. (Optional) Filter by severity: click **Severity** and select "High" or "Critical" to focus on urgent issues
3. (Optional) Filter by date range: click **Date Range** and select the period you want to review
4. Scan the list. Each row shows: Agent name | Tool called | Timestamp | Severity

---

## Step 2: Review the Finding Details

1. Click on the finding you want to review
2. You'll see:
   - **Agent:** Name and ID of the agent that made the call
   - **Tool:** The tool the agent called (e.g., `upgrade_subscription`)
   - **Declared scope:** The tools this agent is allowed to call (if visible)
   - **Timestamp:** When the call was made (ISO 8601 format)
   - **Severity:** Low / Medium / High / Critical
   - **Context:** Why Ridgeline flagged this (e.g., "Tool not in declared scope")
   - **IP address / Source:** Where the call came from (helpful for investigating)

3. Click **View context** to see the API call that triggered the finding (headers, body, response, etc.)

---

## Step 3: Decide What to Do

**Is this an unauthorized call or a false positive?**

- **Unauthorized:** The agent genuinely called a tool it shouldn't have. Go to Step 4.
- **False positive:** The scope declaration is out of date, or there's a legitimate reason the tool was called. Go to Step 5.

### Step 4: Unauthorized — Take Remediation Action

1. Click the **Remediation** button
2. Choose your action:
   - **Revoke access:** Remove this tool from the agent's allowed scope
   - **Update scope declaration:** You'll be directed to your agent management system (not Ridgeline)
3. Confirm the action
4. The finding now shows status **"Remediated"**

### Step 5: False Positive — Mark as Reviewed

1. Click **Mark as reviewed**
2. (Optional) Add a note explaining why this is a false positive (e.g., "Scope updated in v2.3.1"; "Read-only call, no risk")
3. The finding now shows status **"Reviewed"** and won't trigger alerts

## Troubleshooting

### Issue: I see dozens of "low severity" findings. Should I fix them all?

**Why this happens:**  
Low severity usually means the agent called a tool outside scope, but the tool has low risk (e.g., read-only, informational).

**How to handle:**  
Prioritize high and critical findings first. For low severity, consider whether there's a pattern (same agent calling same tool repeatedly) that warrants investigation. Otherwise, monitoring them is acceptable.

### Issue: A finding says the agent called a tool I declared in scope. Why is it flagged?

**Why this happens:**  
Scope might have been updated AFTER the call was made. Findings show the state at the time of the call, not today's state.

**How to handle:**  
Check the finding's timestamp. If it's before your scope update, it's a false positive—mark it reviewed and it won't reappear. If it's after your scope update and it still shows as out-of-scope, contact your admin (there may be a sync delay or data issue).

### Issue: The remediation button is greyed out or I get an error.

**Why this happens:**  
You might not have permission to modify agent scope, or the agent is managed by another team.

**How to handle:**  
Contact the team that manages the agent. You can still mark the finding reviewed and add a note about who needs to take action.

### Issue: I see findings for agents that don't exist anymore.

**Why this happens:**  
Agents can be deactivated or deleted. Old findings remain in the log for audit purposes.

**How to handle:**  
These findings are read-only—you can mark them reviewed if you want to clean up your view, but no remediation action is available. They don't affect your current agents.
